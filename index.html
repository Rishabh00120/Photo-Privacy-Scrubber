EXIF Metadata & Privacy Guide

What is EXIF Metadata?

Exchangeable Image File Format (EXIF) is a standard specification embedded inside image files (primarily JPEG, PNG, and WebP). Whenever a smartphone or digital camera snaps a photo, it automatically records technical, geographic, and temporal parameters into binary headers.

Why GPS Coordinates Pose Severe Privacy Risks

GPS IFD tags embed precise latitude, longitude, and altitude down to fractions of a second. If you share an unscrubbed photo taken at home, school, or work, anyone who downloads the file can extract your exact physical address and pin it to a map.

Camera Details, Timestamps & Unique IDs

Tags such as DateTimeOriginal, Make, Model, BodySerialNumber, and ImageUniqueID disclose your device hardware, routine, and can be used to link separate accounts across online marketplaces, social networks, and forums.

Lossless Byte-Stripping vs Canvas Re-encoding

Mode A (Lossless): Directly parses the file's binary container (JPEG markers, PNG chunks, WebP RIFF) and deletes metadata blocks without touching the compressed image scan data. Zero compression artifacts, identical image quality.

Mode B (Re-encode): Decodes the image onto an HTML5 Canvas and re-encodes a fresh file. This guarantees 100% removal of obscure or malformed metadata, but may slightly recompress pixels.

Understanding Risk Ratings

• HIGH: Exact GPS coordinates or sensitive owner/serial identifiers were detected.
• MEDIUM: Capture timestamps, camera device models, or software details were found.
• LOW: No sensitive fields were found among the inspected tags. Note: Low risk reflects the scope of inspection and does not guarantee the absence of obscure steganographic data.
